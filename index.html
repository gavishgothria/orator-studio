COACH'S PRESCRIPTION: STAGE FRIGHT & ADRENALINE

Reset Your Vagus Nerve in 60 Seconds + Reframe Adrenaline

Your body produces the exact same hormone (adrenaline) for fear and excitement. Instead of trying to "calm down" (which fights biology), tell yourself "My body is energized to perform" and slow your exhale with 4-4-4-4 Box Breathing to lower your heart rate before stepping up.

1. Do 3 rounds of Box Breathing 2. Memorize only your first 2 sentences cold 3. Find 3 friendly faces in the room
01

The 2-Second Power Pause

When you pause for two full seconds after a key point, it feels long to you—but to your audience, it signals supreme authority and lets the message sink in.

02

One Thought, One Pair of Eyes

Don't scan the room like a windshield wiper. Lock eyes with one listener for a full clause or sentence (3–4 seconds), pause, then shift to another section of the room.

03

End Sentences on a Downbeat

Avoid accidental "upspeak" (raising your pitch at the end of declarative statements), which makes statements sound like hesitant questions. Land your final word firmly downward.

30-Second PREP Mental Blueprint

Never start speaking without knowing your landing spot. Jot 3–5 trigger words below during your 30-second prep:

P — Point (First 15s): State your direct answer in one crisp sentence.
R — Reason (Next 20s): Why is this true? Give the core principle.
E — Example (45s): Tell one brief, concrete story or real metric.
P — Point Restated (15s): Tie the story back to your opening thesis.
